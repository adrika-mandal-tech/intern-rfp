# The tasks

Five tasks. The first two are the substance; the last three are real work that also
gives you somewhere useful to go if a task finishes early or stalls.

**Don't start these until you've done the day-one reading** in
[../START_HERE.md](../START_HERE.md) §4.

---

all five.

## Suggested two weeks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Orientation reading. Plus **Task 5 pass 1** — see below, it has to happen today. |
| **Days 2–5** | Task 1. Start with five rows, then stop and check them before doing more. |
| **Days 6–7** | Finish Task 1. Write its summary while it's fresh. |
| **Days 8–10** | Task 2. |
| **Days 11–12** | Task 3 or 4, whichever looks more useful by then. |
| **Remaining** | Task 5 pass 2, and finish anything outstanding. |

**Task 5 pass 1 must happen on day one.** It's ten minutes of notes taken while you
read the explainer for the first time, and it's the only thing in this pack that
becomes impossible to do later — once you understand the project you can't recover what
was confusing about it.

## Order, and why

**Task 1 first, deliberately.** It's self-contained: everything you need ships in this
folder, the method is spelled out, and you can check your own work. You'll finish it
knowing the domain properly, which is what makes Task 2's judgement calls possible —
telling a genuine submitted proposal from a template is much easier once you've read a
real one closely.

**Task 2 second**, because it's the open-ended one. It may not succeed; read its §3
before starting so you know what success looks like at each level.

**Tasks 3–5 are independent.** Take them in any order, or pick one up when another
stalls.

## Two habits that make this work

**Keep `questions.md` running from day one.** Every time something's unclear, a rule
doesn't cover your case, or you disagree with something in this pack — write it down
immediately, even if you resolve it yourself an hour later. Bring the list to check-ins.
The ones you resolved yourself are often the most useful, because they're the gaps in
this pack.

**Log daily.** Three lines: what you did, what you found, what blocked you. Five
minutes, and it's what makes your work reviewable by someone who wasn't watching.

## When to stop and ask

- Stuck for more than half a day on the same thing.
- Two days into Task 2 with nothing in any tier.
- You think something in this pack is wrong. (Write it down either way — that's a
  finding, not an interruption.)
- A rule in Task 1 doesn't cover a case you keep hitting. That's not you missing
  something; it means the rule is incomplete, and we want to know.

Being stuck quietly is the only genuinely expensive mistake available here. Everything
else is recoverable.
