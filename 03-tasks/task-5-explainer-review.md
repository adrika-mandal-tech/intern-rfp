# Task 5 — Read our explainer as a newcomer and tell us where it fails

**Time:** about 1 day · **Deliverable:** a review memo in `your-work/`
**Best done at the very end**, once you know the project — but see §2 about the notes.

---

## 1. The question

`01-context/RFP_AUTOMATION_EXPLAINER.html` was written to explain this project to
people who don't work on it. Whether it succeeds is unknown, because it has only ever
been read by people who already knew everything in it.

You're the first genuine test of it. That makes your confusion **data**, and it has a
short shelf life.

## 2. Do this on day one, then again at the end

Two passes, and the first one is the valuable one:

**Pass 1 — day one, while you still don't know anything.** As you read it the first
time, keep a running note of every place you got lost, re-read a sentence, or hit a term
you didn't know. Don't stop to look things up before writing the note down. Two minutes
of work.

Do this on your first day. Once you understand the project you can never again see what
was confusing about it — you'll read straight past the gap. This is the one part of your
work here that literally cannot be recovered later.

**Pass 2 — at the end.** Re-read it knowing what you now know, and ask the questions in
§3. Now you can tell the difference between "confusing" and "wrong".

## 3. What to look for in pass 2

**Accuracy** — you've read the underlying evidence documents. Does the explainer match
them? Any number that contradicts `02-evidence/`? Any claim stated more confidently
than the evidence supports? **Flag those loudly.** A number that's drifted out of date
is a real defect and finding one is a genuine contribution.

**Gaps** — having done Tasks 1 and 2, is there something important the explainer
doesn't cover? You now know things about this project that its author knew but may not
have written down.

**Structure** — is it in the right order? Anything you needed earlier than it appeared?
Anything that could be cut?

**The plain-language lane specifically** — it's supposed to be readable by someone
non-technical. Test that claim honestly. Where does it slip into jargon without warning?

**The diagrams** — do they help, or are they decoration? Is any of them actually
confusing? A diagram that has to be explained by the text around it isn't working.

## 4. Also review this pack

Same treatment for the folder you've been living in for two weeks:

- Was the reading order in `START_HERE.md` right?
- Did `what-is-an-rfp.md` prepare you for the real documents, or did the PDFs surprise
  you?
- Were the task briefs specific enough to act on? Which one left you guessing?
- **What did you have to ask a person about that should have been written down?** Every
  one of those is a defect in this pack.
- Was anything in here simply wrong?

Be blunt. You're the only person who will ever read these documents without already
knowing the answers, and a polite review is a wasted one.

## 5. Definition of done

A memo in `your-work/`:

- [ ] **Pass 1 notes**, reproduced as written — raw, unedited, in reading order. Don't
      clean these up; the mess is the point.
- [ ] Pass 2 findings, grouped by the §3 headings.
- [ ] Any **accuracy problems** listed first and separately — those are defects, not
      suggestions.
- [ ] A section reviewing this intern pack (§4).
- [ ] Your top five changes, ranked, with a sentence each on why.

## 6. On being critical

This is not a courtesy exercise and there's no risk in being direct. Everything in this
pack was written quickly, by people close to the material, and some of it will be
unclear or wrong. The reason this task exists is that we can't see which parts.

Specific beats polite. "Section 4 lost me at the second paragraph — I didn't know what
'grounding' meant and it wasn't defined until section 7" is worth more than three
paragraphs of balanced assessment.
