# Research memo template

For Tasks 3 and 4. Copy to `your-work/<topic>.md`.

The structure matters more than it looks: **findings and conclusions live in separate
sections.** Someone should be able to read your facts and reach a different conclusion
than you did — that's only possible if the two aren't interleaved.

---

# <Title>

**Author:** · **Date:** · **Task:**

## 1. The question

What you were asked to find out, in two or three sentences, in your own words. If you
interpreted the brief in a particular way, say so here.

## 2. Summary

Five to ten bullets. What someone gets if they read nothing else.

Lead with the finding that most changes what a reader would do, not with the easiest
thing to state.

## 3. Findings

The substance. **Facts and sources only** — save your interpretation for §4.

Every claim carries a source and an access date. Use whatever sub-structure fits the
task; one section per question group in the brief usually works.

Mark clearly which claims are:

- **Verified** — you saw it directly and can point at it
- **Reported** — a source says so and you couldn't independently confirm it
- **Vendor claim** — a company says it about its own product

That distinction is doing real work. "Responsive says it supports gap detection" and
"Responsive supports gap detection" are very different statements, and only one of them
is supportable from a product page.

## 4. Interpretation

Now your reading of the above. Clearly separated, clearly labelled as yours.

What do the findings mean for the decision this feeds? What surprised you? What would
you push back on if you heard someone assert the opposite?

An honest opinion is wanted here. By the end of one of these tasks you'll have read more
about the topic than anyone else on the project — a neutral summary throws that away.

## 5. What I could not determine

Specific gaps, each with what you tried. This section being empty is a warning sign, not
a good result — two or three days of research on an open question always leaves loose
ends, and hiding them just moves the surprise downstream.

## 6. Sources

Numbered list. URL, title, access date, and one line on what it gave you.

Note which sources were marketing material — see Task 3 §5 and Task 4 §7.
